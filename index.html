Playing the farm

How the pieces fit together

Claim and build

Choose an available plot and claim it before changing the farm. Place up to six free starter soil beds, then use the owned Build board to preview, rotate, place, move or cancel an object. Moving an owned building does not charge a new construction cost. Empty soil may be stored; planted soil must be harvested first. New buildings still require their level, coins, materials and timer. Only selected first-contact and building routes have native Play proof.

Plant and harvest

Buy the appropriate seed or sapling, plant it on empty owned soil, and wait for the current compact timer shown in the catalogue. Harvesting grants the listed crop quantity, XP and a replacement planting supply when storage has room. An immature plant cannot be collected early. Wheat has a complete selected local cycle; the other crop routes need broader acceptance.

Store and upgrade

The barn and silo represent different storage paths, and the guide lists four tiered upgrades. The server owns capacity, costs and inventory changes. Collection that would overflow storage is rejected rather than silently losing goods. The silo's physical route has selected local proof; the full split-storage and upgrade paths remain in development.

Make goods

Construct an owned station, supply a recipe's exact ingredients, queue it, wait for its timer and collect the output. The catalogue shows all 13 recipes and their station, level, input and yield. The feedmill and chicken-feed loop has selected local evidence. The rest are source-defined production plans until earned and published paths are exercised.

Care for animals

Construct owned housing, reach the animal's level and price, purchase it through its physical route, make its matching feed, then feed and collect after the timer. Animals and products belong to the exact farm owner. A chicken purchase, feeding and egg collection have been observed locally; cow, sheep and pig routes remain in development.

Orders, sales and visitors

Physical boards describe requests and rewards before an authoritative transaction removes goods or pays coins and XP. The town's visitor services use owned buildings and timed visits. Trading needs durable settlement and two-account isolation; those live guarantees have not yet passed. No public guide card treats a defined order or visitor route as already verified online.

Coins and diamonds

Coins come from earned actions and pay for seeds, construction, animals and upgrades. Diamonds are a separate convenience currency with confirmation before spending. Developer-product diamond packs are disabled until their actual products and receipt handling are verified. The guide does not offer a purchase button.

Saving and release

Studio uses session-only profiles by design. A published server must use durable storage, report its real save status and preserve earned progress after a full leave and rejoin. That live acceptance remains open. The Roblox beta link will appear only when a specific public version passes fresh join, control and persistence checks.

Original world lore

A town built one useful thing at a time

The valley began with a shared arrival hall and empty fields. New farmers did not inherit a finished estate. They chose a plot, set down their first soil, and learned what the land would return when they gave it time.

Wheat paid for the first barn. The barn made room for animals; the feedmill gave those animals a daily rhythm. The depot, market, bakery, and other buildings grew around the same idea: a town becomes real when the people in it can use its doors, work its machines, and bring something home.

The old equipment in the depot is kept because its makers left practical lessons behind. Every shutter, counter, and room has a job. The town's story is still being written by ordinary harvests, careful construction, and visitors who can finally stay awhile.

This is original setting fiction for the guide. It does not claim that unfinished quests, characters, or buildings are already playable.
